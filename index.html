Continuous Usability Engineering

持续易用性工程（中文名）· 中文

noun, emerging term. The engineering practice of treating usability — whether people can find their way through shipped software — as a continuously measured and governed property: periodic measurement cycles against a held-constant task set, cross-version regression ledgers for discovered issues, human adjudication of severity and acceptance, and release gates that consume usability readings. Organized as an institutional function — a named role, a budget line, an owned ledger — in the way Site Reliability Engineering organizes reliability.

Character

  • Deficit-driven, not test-first. The repair queue is driven by gaps discovered in measurement rounds, not by assertions declared in advance (what gets measured is a human-governed choice, not an emergent one). Numbers declared without an anchor may inform intent but never gate a release; a threshold gates only on an anchor — derived by analysis and signed off by a person, drawn from measurement history (one's own, or another's within a stated validity domain), or set by regulation, which gates as compliance and never as evidence of what users tolerate.
  • Cycles, not sessions. The unit of work is a measurement round; round N's readings are round N+1's prescription input. Quantitative targets are warm-started from measurement history rather than cold-started before any data exists — continuity is what makes quantification affordable. Affordability is bounded, not a promise of getting cheaper: it removes the cost of rediscovery, while the cost of holding the ledger (registry, guards, re-anchoring) does not decline.
  • Human adjudication is a load-bearing invariant. Severity judgment and issue acceptance remain human decisions as the practice scales; they are not mechanized away.

Genealogy

Inherited, with deliberate modification, from Usability Engineering (Tyldesley 1988; Nielsen 1993). The ancestor's quantitative core — usability as a measurable engineering quantity, tested after construction — is retained in full. What is modified is its unconditional front-loading of quantitative targets: the practice front-loads only what is analytically derivable and human-signed-off (semantic floors: required information × required decisions), and anchors tolerance bands in measurement history instead.

From Site Reliability Engineering it takes the institutional form, not the domain: formation by having software engineers design the verification function; a named role and budget line rather than a project ritual; regression ledgers and gate policy rather than one-off studies. In one line — the ancestor said “set quantitative goals, build, then test”; this practice says “measure first, then quantify; derive first, then prescribe; run continuously so that quantification and prescription regenerate each cycle.”

Scope and neighbors

The term names the engineering-gate layer of mainstream software practice. The vacancy it fills is claimed narrowly: in mainstream software the mechanism layer is mature (CI release gates, metrics, and analytics are abundant), while task-usability metric families appear in no release-gate condition. This is not a claim that the industry lacks usability governance. Adjacent and vertical occupants are recorded, not denied:

  • Games User Research (IGDA GUR SIG) — a same-layer vertical precedent in the games industry.
  • IEC 62366 usability engineering — the regulated vertical (medical devices).
  • Continuous UX measurement, product analytics, ResearchOps — the measurement-and-discourse layer.

Mainstream software demonstrably accommodates governance disciplines with institutional slots — accessibility engineering is the worked example — which is what makes this slot shape credible. The term is not a software development methodology (not a member of the *-DD family), and not a subtype of software testing: its measuring object is whether people find their way, not whether the machine is correct.

Disambiguation

  • The initialism “CUE” is occupied by Molich's Comparative Usability Evaluation series (1998–2018, ten rounds) — an unrelated long-running series of usability-evaluation-method comparison studies. External material for the practice on this page uses the full name and avoids the initialism.
  • Not “continuous use” — retention and engagement analytics.
  • Chinese register note: the Chinese name 持续易用性工程 (“continuous ease-of-use engineering”) deliberately prefers 易用性 (ease of use) over the more literal academic translation 可用性 (usability). In Chinese software-engineering usage 可用性 first reads as availability (uptime, high availability), and 持续可用 (“continuously available”) would misland exactly in SRE's territory. The variant 持续可用性工程 also circulates in internal and academic registers; both names refer to this practice.

Status and provenance

This page is a terminology placeholder and genealogy record, first published 2026-11-17. The practice described here is in early use by its initial practitioner. The phrase itself has appeared before in the software-engineering literature as a descriptive label — a glossary entry in a 2026 review (Carvalho et al., Journal of Software: Evolution and Process, doi:10.1002/smr.70161); the definition above is a working definition and may evolve (see changelog). External adoption is the test that matters for any engineering discipline, and has not yet occurred. Source, edit history, and issue tracker: github.com/continuous-usability-engineering.
